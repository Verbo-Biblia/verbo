How to please God today?

When Scripture speaks of pleasing God, it does not present it as an emotional state that the believer must provoke in Him, but as a life that harmonizes with His character. It is not about manipulating a divine reaction; It is about walking in coherence with who He is.

Many Christians want to please the Lord. They read the Bible, they pray, they sing, they listen to devotionals, they meditate on Him. And in different spiritual seasons, they can find themselves in moments of greater strength, greater clarity, greater spiritual sensitivity. In such times, a deep and sincere question arises: how does one truly please God?

1. Without faith it is impossible to please him

The base text is forceful:

"Without faith it is impossible to please God; for he who draws near to God must believe that he exists, and that he is a rewarder of those who seek him." Hebrews 11:6

Scripture does not say: without emotional intensity, without ministerial production, without moral perfection. He says without faith.

But biblical faith is not simply believing that God exists. The same verse adds that He is a rewarder of those who seek Him. It is an active trust, a living dependence.

In a season of spiritual prosperity, what really pleases God is not that the believer is strong, but that he recognizes that that strength is not his. When someone can humbly say, "He is sustaining me more than I am," there is something deeply pleasing before the Lord.

Because self-sufficiency offends. Dependence honors.

2. Pleasing God is not perfection, it is direction

Paul warns:

"So let him who thinks he stands take heed that he does not fall." 1 Corinthians 10:12

And it also teaches:

"Let them walk worthy of the Lord, pleasing Him in every way." Colossians 1:10

Note the language: walk. Don't "never stumble." Don't "never have fights." Walk.

The evidence of salvation is not perfection, it is continued repentance: "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves... but if we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive" (1 John 1:8–9).

God doesn't smile because someone didn't fall today. God delights when the heart is soft, teachable, quick to return to Him.

It is not impeccability. It is direction. It is a heart that walks towards God even when it stumbles.

3. What concrete things please God?

The Scripture is clear and surprisingly simple.

Simple obedience.

"Surely to obey is better than sacrifices; and to heed than the fat of rams." 1 Samuel 15:22

These are not heroic acts or spectacular demonstrations. It is about concrete obedience in everyday life.

Practical love. Jesus said:

"By this everyone will know that they are my disciples, if they have love for one another." John 13:35

Pleasing God includes how you treat your wife, how you shepherd your children, how you respond when no one is looking. Many times we want to please God in the "vertical", but He measures the vertical by the horizontal:

"If anyone says, I love God, and hates his brother, he is a liar." 1 John 4:20

Modesty.

"God resists the proud, and gives grace to the humble." James 4:6

Humility is not denying the work of God in one. It's giving him all the credit. It is walking with fear and trembling, recognizing that all steadfastness is sustained by grace.

Remain in Christ. Jesus declared:

"I am the vine, you are the branches: he who is in me, and I in him, he bears much fruit; for without me you can do nothing." John 15:5

The fruit is not self-made. It is a result of permanence. God likes us to depend, not to produce.

4. The subtle error: wanting to "impress" God

In seasons of spiritual strength there is a fine temptation: wanting to maintain the level. Convert holiness into performance.

There the simplicity of the Gospel is lost. The focus is not on “maintaining my spiritual state,” but on remaining in Christ.

The Father is not impressed with human performance. The Father delights in the Son. And when the believer is in the Son, he is in that which pleases the Father most.

Ephesians chapter one teaches that we are accepted "in the Beloved." That means something deeply liberating: the Father's smile rests first on Christ. And the believer participates in that smile because he is united to Him.

5. So... how to please the Lord?

In simple, biblical terms: trust when you don't understand, obey when it costs, repent quickly when you fail, love those He has placed close, don't lose your amazement, don't take credit for what He does. And above all: enjoy God more than your own spiritual state.

If someone starts enjoying their "spiritual bonanza", they become distracted. If you enjoy God Himself, you are exactly where you need to be.

6. A truth that liberates

The believer is not trying to make God start smiling. He is living under a God who has already taken full pleasure in His Son.

The holy life does not buy His pleasure. It is a response to a pleasure that was already manifested in Christ.

And when you walk in that consciousness—with fear, yes, but also with joy—that is deeply pleasing before Him.

Pleasing God is not impressing heaven. It is remaining in Christ with a humble, dependent and obedient heart. And in that simplicity, the Father delights.
