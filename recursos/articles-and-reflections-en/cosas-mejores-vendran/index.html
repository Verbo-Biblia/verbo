The error in the phrase "better things will come"

We live in a time marked by constant restlessness: the feeling that "something more" is always missing. Something more in work, something more in the economy, something more in ministry, something more in spiritual life. This mentality seems innocent, but it reveals a distorted way of measuring greatness. We have learned to call "small" that which actually sustains life.

We often say we value the little things, but at the same time we dream of something bigger: more success, more recognition, more impact. However, when we look honestly, we discover that what we call small—life, health, family, peace in the home, daily food—is actually immensely large. They are the gifts that make existence itself possible.

Scripture expresses it with profound simplicity:

"And also that it is the gift of God that every man eat and drink, and enjoy the good of all his work." Ecclesiastes 3:13

It is not presented as a simple rest while we wait for something more important; It is presented as part of the Lord's very goodness towards us:

"Behold then the good thing that I have seen: That what is good is to eat and drink, and to enjoy the good of all the work with which he toils under the sun, all the days of his life that God has given him; for this is his part." Ecclesiastes 5:18

Everyday life, when lived with gratitude, becomes a space where God's grace becomes visible.

Something similar happens in the spiritual life. Many times we wish that God would use us in something extraordinary, something that will be noticed. But biblical Christianity is not defined by the spectacular, but by fidelity. Remaining in Christ, walking with Him in everyday life, sustaining faith in silence, loving family, living with integrity when no one is looking: these are often the most profound ways in which God works in the lives of His people.

In the end, the problem is not that God hasn't given us "something else." The problem is that we have lost the correct scale of what is big. When we recover the biblical perspective we discover that life itself is already a gift, that peace is a mercy, that the family is an inheritance, and that the greatest grace of all is that God has given us his Son:

"He who did not spare his own Son, but gave him up for us all, how will he not also give us all things with him?" Romans 8:32

If we have Christ, we have everything. And when that truth is understood, even the things we call simple—a quiet day, a shared table, a conversation with those we love—stop seeming small and we realize the magnitude of those blessings, which are more than enough reasons to live with gratitude and give glory to God.
