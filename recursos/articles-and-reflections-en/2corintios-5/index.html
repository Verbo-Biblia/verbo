2 Corinthians 5:15-17: new creature, new way of seeing

The apostle Paul's second letter to the Corinthians, chapter five, from verses fifteen to seventeen, describes a radical change in our way of seeing.

"And he died for all, so that those who live should no longer live for themselves, but for him who died and was resurrected for them. So that we from now on know no one according to the flesh: and even if we knew Christ according to the flesh, but now we know him no more. Therefore if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creature: old things have passed away; behold, all things have become new." 2 Corinthians 5:15-17

Paul affirms that because Christ died for all, those who live no longer live for themselves, but for Him who died and rose again for them. Therefore, from now on we know no one according to the flesh, and this includes even the way we previously understood Christ. The old, merely human way of looking and evaluating is left behind.

Being in Christ does not just mean forgiveness; means reorientation. A new creature is not simply someone who acts differently, but someone who perceives differently. We no longer judge our brothers with the criteria that we used when we walked in the flesh, nor do we reduce them to what they were when they themselves walked in the flesh. Your past is no longer your definition, because Christ does not relate to us that way.

Even our way of speaking about Christ has been transformed. We no longer do it from a human, limited and earthly perspective, but from a renewed perspective, born of revelation. Everything is interpreted in light of what God has done in Him and through Him.

Being a new creature defines not only our relationship with Christ, but also our relationship with our brothers. It involves a new nature, new concepts, new motivations, a new perspective and a new criterion, even in dealing with the weaknesses and defects of others. Being in Christ is a profound redefinition of how we see, how we speak, how we embrace and how we love: we learn to love like Christ, because in Him, truly, we are made new.
