His faithfulness, not mine

There is something that many believers still cannot fully rest on… and it is this: our faithfulness does not begin with us, it begins with God.

We live in constant tension, trying to be faithful, trying not to fail, trying to do the right thing... and when we fail, we feel that everything is shaking, that everything is breaking, that God is moving away. But Scripture shows us another completely different reality.

"If we are unfaithful, he remains faithful: he cannot deny himself." 2 Timothy 2:13

That means there is something deeper than your effort... there is a faithfulness that sustains you, even when you are weak.

And this is where that clear example comes in: a covenant relationship, like marriage. Not a contract based on performance, but a commitment that remains even when there is weakness. But with God it is even greater, because it is not only a pact... it is a pact that He Himself maintains.

"Being confident of this, that He who began a good work in you will carry it to completion until the day of Jesus Christ." Philippians 1:6

So you are not walking alone… you are being held.

And here comes something that frees the soul in a profound way: it is not that God barely tolerates you... it is that God accepts you in the Beloved.

"To the praise of the glory of his grace, whereby he hath made us accepted in the Beloved." Ephesians 1:6

That completely changes the way you approach God. Because now, when you fail... you don't run far. When you fail... you don't hide. When you fail... you don't condemn yourself as if you were no longer worthy.

Now you do something different: you come back. And that return… that is fidelity.

"If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and there is no truth in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and cleanse us from all unrighteousness." 1 John 1:8-9

Look at this clearly: even at the moment you fail, if you draw closer to God… you are not being a hypocrite. You are being faithful. Because the unfaithful is not the one who falls... the unfaithful is the one who stays away. But the son... returns.

And that's where Romans 5:1 really comes to life:

"Therefore justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ." Romans 5:1

It doesn't say that you will have peace when you do everything right. It doesn't say that you will have peace when you stop failing. He says you have peace…now.

That means you are not at war with God. You're not trying to earn their approval. You are not walking a spiritual tightrope. You are at peace. You are son. You are accepted.

And from that place... you live. From that place you pray. From that place you fight against sin. From that place you get up when you fall.

And something happens when the heart truly understands this… you no longer want to play with sin. You don't want to walk away anymore. You no longer want to treat grace as something light. Not because you are afraid... but because you have known love.

Fidelity then stops being a burden… and becomes a response. A response to a God who did not let you go, who did not reject you, who did not abandon you, but who received you completely in Christ.

So today, if you have failed... if you have felt far away... if you have thought that you are not worthy of reaching out... do the most faithful thing you can do: return to Him.

Because your faithfulness is not what sustains the relationship... it is His faithfulness that allows you to remain.

And in that place... there is rest for the soul.
