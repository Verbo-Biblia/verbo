Ephesians 2: From strangers to family, by grace alone

Ephesians 2, from verse eleven on, forces us to look back to understand who we are today.

Paul asks us to remember—not to live anchored to the past, but to not forget grace:

"Who at that time were without Christ, alienated from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers to the covenants of promise, without hope and without God in the world. But now in Christ Jesus, you who once were far away have been made near by the blood of Christ." Ephesians 2:12-13

Before we were not a people, we had no spiritual identity, we were without hope and without God in the world. It was not just a moral condition; It was an existential condition: outside the pact, outside the promise, outside of life.

But that "before" was broken by the cross. In Christ, those who were far away were brought near. Not by merit, not by spiritual progress, but by the blood of Christ. The cross not only forgives; redefines. There God creates a new people, reconciled with Him and with each other:

"So they are no longer foreigners or aliens, but citizens together with the saints, and householders of God." Ephesians 2:19

We are no longer strangers or aliens, but members of God's family.

That connects directly with what Peter expresses: "You are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy people, a special people [...] who in the past were not a people, but now are the people of God" (1 Peter 2:9-10). Not because we always were, but precisely because we weren't. Our identity is not born from what we brought, but from what Christ did. Remembering what we were protects the heart from pride and keeps gratitude alive.

Ephesians 2 teaches us that Christian identity is not a religious title, but a new belonging. Before we were "nothing" in terms of pact; Now we are a people. And that people does not exist to exalt themselves, but to live as a living testimony that God takes those who were not and makes them his own by pure grace.
