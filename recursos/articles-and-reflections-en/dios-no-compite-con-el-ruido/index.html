God does not compete with noise

Sometimes we think that the problem with our spiritual life is that we don't want to pray or that we don't want to read the Bible. But if we're honest, most believers would say they do. There is a real desire. He who is born again does not reject God, he does not flee from Him by nature; There is something inside that inclines him, that attracts him, that calls him.

So the problem is not as simple as saying "I don't want to."

Scripture speaks of the weakness of the flesh, and that is a reality. But it also says something more delicate, something that we often overlook:

"But put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and do not pay attention to the flesh in your desires." Romans 13:14

That is to say, not only is there an internal struggle, but there is also a way of living that constantly fuels that struggle.

And that's where we are today.

We live in a time where there are almost no breaks. Any free space is immediately filled. If there is silence, we break it. If there is a wait, we avoid it. If there is a moment without stimulus, we replace it with content. We don't realize it, but we have learned not to be still.

Before, human beings lived with empty moments: long walks without distraction, waiting times, silence at home, even the simple act of sitting without doing anything. And in those spaces, almost without trying, the soul opened up. The mind reflected. The heart was inclined towards God.

Today that has almost disappeared.

We are constantly connected, but not necessarily connected to reality. We are connected to a screen. And the more we fill ourselves with stimuli, the harder it becomes to stop. And without realizing it, we began to live without those spaces where prayer would naturally arise, where the Word would take place, where praise would flow effortlessly.

Then something happens that can confuse us: we want God, but we can't find the time. It's not that there is no desire, it's that there is no space.

"But you, when you pray, go into your chamber, and shut your door, and pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you openly." Matthew 6:6

It is not just an external instruction. It is an invitation to step away, to create a place where other things do not interfere. Because God is not in the midst of the constant noise of a saturated life.

He himself separated:

"But he withdrew into the deserts and prayed." Luke 5:16

Not because I didn't have things to do, but precisely because I did. In the midst of the demand, He created space.

Perhaps the problem is not that the meat is too strong, but that we have learned to feed it without pause. And at the same time, we have stopped feeding the spirit, not so much because of rejection, but because we do not leave room for it.

And in the midst of all this, the voice of Scripture continues to speak clearly.

"For my people have committed two evils: they have left me, the fountain of living water, to dig cisterns for themselves, broken cisterns that can hold no water." Jeremiah 2:13

It is not that human beings have stopped searching, it is that they have searched in places that do not satisfy.

"Martha, Martha, you are careful, and with many things you are troubled: but one thing is necessary; and Mary chose the good part, which will not be taken away from her." Luke 10:41-42

It is not that what we do is bad in itself, it is that it can take the place of what is essential.

"Be still, and know that I am God." Psalm 46:10

But if we are never still, how will we know?

"Come close to God, and he will come close to you." James 4:8

He is not far away. But approaching means stopping, it means moving in the opposite direction to the constant flow of distraction.

"For where your treasure is, there your heart will be." Matthew 6:21

And today, many times, our time and our attention reveal more than we would like to admit.

It's not that we don't love God. It's not that we don't want to look for it. Our lives are so full that we don't know where to find them.

God does not compete with noise. God reveals himself in silence.
