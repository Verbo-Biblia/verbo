"It's not the end yet": discerning without speculating

We live in times when world events can trouble the heart. Wars, pandemics, earthquakes, economic crises, and rapid change raise legitimate questions, with many asking, “Is this the end?”

Jesus himself answered that question. And he did it with clarity, with love and with deep pastoral care.

When His disciples asked Him about the end, Jesus did not leave them without an answer, but He did not leave them trapped in fear either. He spoke of real and visible signs:

"And you will hear wars, and rumors of wars: see that you are not troubled; for all this must come to pass; but the end is not yet. For nation will rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom; and there will be pestilences, and famines, and earthquakes in the places. And all these things, the beginning of sorrows." Matthew 24:6-8

He did not deny any of these things. Jesus called these realities "beginning of pains," like the labor pains before a birth. They are real, they are painful, they are intense, but they are not the end.

Throughout history, the church—often with good intentions—has repeatedly fallen into the same mistake: calling “the end” what Jesus clearly said was not the end. At different times it was stated that the world would end around the year 1000, during great plagues such as the Black Death, in the midst of world wars, with the turn of the millennium in the year 2000 or recently during the COVID-19 pandemic. In each of those moments it was said: "now yes", and yet, the end did not come.

This does not mean that Jesus was wrong. It means that we misinterpret what He had already explained.

Jesus never called us to ignore the times, but neither did he call us to live alarmed or to put signs where He did not put them. He clearly said that these things would happen and that is precisely why he warned: "See that no one deceives you" (Matthew 24:4).

Jesus' purpose was not to produce fear, but peace in the midst of chaos. It was not to distract ourselves with speculation, but to focus on the mission. Because Jesus also clearly defined what marks the end:

"And this gospel of the kingdom will be preached in all the world, as a testimony to all the Gentiles; and then the end will come." Matthew 24:14

Not when there is another war. Not when another pandemic arises. Not when another earthquake hits. Jesus' emphasis was never on the calendar, but on the message.

Therefore, when the disciples asked him again about the times, Jesus told them with love and firmness: "It is not for you to know the times or the seasons that the Father has placed in his sole power" (Acts 1:7). Not because they don't matter, but because they shouldn't rule our hearts.

Our calling is not to live in anxiety, but in hope. It is not distracting ourselves with theories, but walking faithfully with Christ. And when the day of the end arrives—because it will—it will not be a cause for terror, but for joy, knowing that we were faithful to the task that He entrusted to us.

We discern without speculating. Let us be attentive without alarming ourselves. Let's live in peace, not in fear. Let us love the return of the Lord without neglecting the message that we must announce today.

Jesus did not leave us without signs. But it also didn't leave us without an excuse to invent them.
