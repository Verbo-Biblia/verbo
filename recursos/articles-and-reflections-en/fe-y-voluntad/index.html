When it is not faith that is broken, but will

There are times when we are not doubting God. We are not questioning Christ or denying the way. What breaks, sometimes, is not faith... it is will.

There are seasons we believe in, but we don't want to. We continue walking, but without the strength to sustain the purpose. We have not decided to give up, but neither do we feel the desire to persevere firmly.

Scripture is no stranger to this. Elijah believed, but he went so far as to say that he no longer wanted to live:

"And he went into the wilderness a day's journey, and came and sat under a juniper tree; and wishing to die, he said, Enough now, O Lord, take away my soul; for I am no better than my fathers." 1 Kings 19:4

Jeremiah was faithful, but he cursed the day he was born because of the weight of his calling (Jeremiah 20:14-18). Job did not deny God, but he lost the meaning of his own existence: "After this Job opened his mouth, and cursed his day" (Job 3:1). Ezekiel remained silent for seven days, astonished at the ruin of his people (Ezekiel 3:15). Paul confessed that he was afflicted inside and out, to the point of thinking that they would not survive, and that his only comfort was God Himself:

"For, brothers, we do not want you to be unaware of our tribulation that was done to us in Asia; that we were burdened beyond measure in such a way that we were in doubt of life. But we had in ourselves the answer of death, so that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God who raises the dead." 2 Corinthians 1:8-9

None of them were rebuked by God. None were discarded. God did not demand a will from them when they no longer had it. God sustained them.

Sometimes there is a gap in faith. Sometimes it is a gap in the will to continue, to persevere in the purpose. It is not abandoning the path, it is not having the strength to carry it. And yet, God does not leave.

God does not ask us if he can sustain us. He doesn't ask us for permission to hold hands. He does it. And over time we discovered that, even when we didn't want anything, He was always there.

Sometimes it's not that my faith has cracks. Sometimes it is my will that breaks. I don't stop believing, but I stop loving. I don't abandon the path, but I lose my purpose. And yet, God does not leave. He doesn't ask me if he can hold me. It just does it. And over time I discover that, even in the silence, He was always there.
