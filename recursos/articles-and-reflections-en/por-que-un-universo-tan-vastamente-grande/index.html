Why such a vastly large universe?

Reflections on the glory and pleasure of God.

Introduction: the magnitude of the universe and the glory of God

A common question among believers and skeptics is: if there is only life on Earth, why did God create such a vast universe? The Bible gives us a fundamental answer: everything that exists, exists for the glory of God. The psalmist declares:

"The heavens declare the glory of God, and the firmament proclaims the work of his hands." Psalm 19:1

And in another passage, the same awe turns into humble adoration:

"When I contemplate your heavens, the work of your fingers, the moon and the stars that you fixed there, I ask myself: What is man, that you think of him? What is the human being, that you take him into account?" Psalm 8:3–4

The immense size of the cosmos is not a coincidence: it is the mirror of God's greatness. The more we understand the vastness of the universe, the more we understand our smallness and His infinite glory.

Common questions and objections

Some wonder: "Wouldn't a smaller universe have been enough to show His power? Even one ten times smaller would still be impressive." Others, from skepticism, say that such a large universe proves that God does not exist or that life must have emerged on other planets.

But these objections are based on two very limited human assumptions: that the glory of God is to be appreciated only by human beings, and that creation exists primarily for our observation. However, Scripture reveals a much deeper reality: the universe was not created just for us, but also for the delight of God Himself—and for the worship of beings we cannot see, such as angels (Job 38:7).

God's delight in His creation

God does not create out of necessity, but for pleasure. Not just to be admired, but because he enjoys His own work. The book of Revelation proclaims:

"You created all things; by your will they exist and were created." Revelation 4:11

The Greek word translated "will" (thelēma) can also be understood as desire or pleasure, which shows us that creation is an expression of divine joy. God exists eternally in three Persons—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—and creation itself is the fruit of Trinitarian joy. The universe not only reflects His power, but also His inner joy. Every galaxy, every particle, is part of a symphony that God enjoys in His own perfection.

Life beyond Earth: an admirable possibility

Some think that the possibility of life outside Earth contradicts the wisdom of God, but in reality, it does not threaten it, but rather magnifies it. If the Creator delights in creating, why be surprised if His creativity goes beyond what we can see? Discovering life in unexpected places—like in the subterranean oceans of Europa, a moon of Jupiter—would be as wonderful as discovering new species deep beneath the Earth. None of this contradicts the Gospel, because all life remains His, created for His glory.

Creation as a declaration of glory

The vastness of the universe is not cosmic waste; It is a visual declaration of the power, creativity and pleasure of God. God is His own audience: He Himself contemplates and takes pleasure in what He has made. Every galaxy and every planet is a reflection of His inexhaustible power. The immensity of the cosmos does not reduce the importance of humanity, but rather magnifies the amazement that such an immense God has decided to love us.

As the prophet Isaiah wrote:

"Lift up your eyes and look at the heavens: Who created all this? He who arranges the multitude of the stars one by one, and calls each one by name." Isaiah 40:26

Conclusion: a universe that reflects the greatness of God

In short: the immensity of the universe is not evidence of absence, but of divine abundance. Creation is not a waste of space, but a manifestation of His glory and pleasure. God delights in His work, even when no one sees it. And if there were life beyond Earth, it would be just another stage where His genius shines.

The universe is not big by accident: it is big because it reflects an infinitely glorious God, whose joy fills all creation. And just as the heavens declare His glory, the cross declares His love: the same God who spread the galaxies stooped to redeem us. Every star, every planet, and every living being are reminders that the entire universe is an offering of glory to the Creator, and an invitation to join the chorus that never ceases:

"Holy, Holy, Holy is the Lord God Almighty, who was, who is, and who is to come!" Revelation 4:8
