Romans 8: security in Christ, not absence of pain

Romans 8 reminds us who we are and where we are going.

Paul affirms a present and firm truth:

"Now therefore there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, who walk not according to the flesh, but according to the spirit." Romans 8:1

That security is not based on a life without stumbling blocks, but on a direction of the heart: walking according to the Spirit, even when the flesh continues to show its fragility, as Paul himself recognizes in chapter seven.

From that identity, current suffering is relocated. It is not denied or minimized, but it is declared provisional:

"For I am certain that what is suffered at this time cannot be compared with the glory to come that will be manifested in us." Romans 8:18

The sufferings of now have no real weight compared to future glory; a glory so great that it does not admit comparison or complete explanation. The pain is real, but not definitive.

And the closing is forceful:

"Who will separate us from the love of Christ? tribulation? or distress? or persecution? or famine? or nakedness? or danger? or knife? [...] For I am certain that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor height, nor depth, nor any creature will be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord." Romans 8:35, 38-39

Nothing can separate us from the love of Christ. No failures, no tests, no anguish, no uncertain future. Romans 8 does not promise an easy life, but it does promise a secure life: sustained by the love of God, affirmed in Christ and guided by the Spirit, with hope placed in a glory that is coming and that surpasses everything that hurts today.
