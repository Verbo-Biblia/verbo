Kindness is not the same as condescension to sin

The Christian life is marked by piety, grace, mercy and a serene character. The believer was not called to be harsh or confrontational. But we must understand something clearly: it is one thing to reflect the character of Christ, and quite another to be condescending to sin.

Many times, in trying to be kind, we end up giving in where we shouldn't. We don't participate directly, but we stay. We don't say anything, but we smile. We don't approve, but we don't make a difference either. And little by little, so as not to make us uncomfortable, we begin to live with environments that do not honor God.

"Let love be without pretense: hating what is evil, drawing near to what is good." Romans 12:9

It doesn't say "tolerate the bad so as not to offend." He says he hates it.

The problem is that we confuse grace with permissiveness. But grace is not complicity. Jesus showed mercy, but never validated sin:

"Neither do I condemn you: go, and sin no more." John 8:11

He loved the person, but he did not soften the truth.

This is where many believers fall without realizing it. We think that as long as we don't participate, we're fine. But the Bible also warns about permanence:

"Blessed is the man who does not walk in the counsel of the wicked, nor stand in the way of sinners, nor sit in the seat of scorners." Psalm 1:1

It's not just what you do, it's where you stay.

And this is serious, because it becomes a silent danger. If we do not discern, we begin to get used to it. What used to bother us stops doing so. What we previously rejected, we tolerate. And without realizing it, we end up participating in things we wouldn't want to.

That is why the Word warns:

"And do not communicate with the fruitless works of darkness, but rather rebuke them." Ephesians 5:11

It doesn't just say "don't practice them," but don't participate. And also:

"Make no mistake: bad conversations corrupt good customs." 1 Corinthians 15:33

It doesn't just talk about acts, but about environments.

Now, it is important to clarify: this does not mean isolating ourselves from society. It is not about leaving work, studies, neighbors or all relationships with others. Nor is it about confronting everything at all times or adopting an aggressive or superior attitude.

It's about discerning moments.

There are spaces where we can be light, show grace and speak with wisdom. But there are moments where the environment stops being an opportunity and becomes pollution. And there the believer must have the clarity to say: I do not belong here.

"Therefore come out from among them, and be separate, says the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing: and I will receive you." 2 Corinthians 6:17

It is not fleeing from the world, it is separating from what contaminates within the world.

Because the real danger is this: wanting to maintain an appearance of piety without paying the price of being different. And that produces an ambiguous Christianity: it does not fully participate, but it does not depart either. He doesn't approve, but he doesn't distinguish himself either.

And that ends up affecting holiness. Holiness is not only lost when one falls openly, but when one becomes accustomed to what displeases God.

And when that happens, the Spirit begins to bother. We feel bad. We realize that something is not right. And we understand that we need to go back, readjust, cleanse, get away again.

Therefore, true mercy is not a kindness that avoids discomfort. It is a life that loves people, but does not accommodate sin.

"For am I now persuading men or God? Or do I seek to please men? Certainly, if I still pleased men, I would not be a servant of Christ." Galatians 1:10

That is the key question.

God's grace teaches us to love truly, to live purely, and to maintain a clear difference. Not an aggressive difference, nor a proud one, but a holy difference.

Because the Christian was not called to fit in… he was called to remain faithful.
