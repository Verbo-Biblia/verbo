The desert is not the exception, it is the way

Many times we think that the standard of life is to be well. And when something hurts, when we go through a difficult stage, we usually say: "I went through a desert", as if the desert were just a specific event, a bad week or an exceptional moment.

But Scripture corrects that idea. The Bible clearly says, in Ecclesiastes chapter three, that everything has its season:

"For all things there is a season, and everything desired under heaven has its time: a time to be born, and a time to die; a time to plant, and a time to uproot what is planted [...] a time to cry, and a time to laugh; a time to mourn, and a time to dance." Ecclesiastes 3:1-4

This teaches us that difficult stages are not an anomaly in life, but part of the human walk... and also of the Christian walk.

The life of the believer is very similar to the experience of the people of Israel. Israel was rescued from slavery, crossed the desert and headed to the promised land. But on that path not everything was easy: there were wars, there was hunger, there was thirst, there was uncertainty. And yet, God never abandoned them.

The pillar of cloud and fire was always there: "And the Lord went before them by day in a pillar of cloud to guide them on the way; and by night in a pillar of fire to give them light" (Exodus 13:21). The water that came out of the rock was there (Exodus 17:6). The manna fell every day (Exodus 16:4). God's provision was not lacking... but the road was still a desert.

That is also the life of the believer today. The believer does not live in a permanent oasis. The believer is not yet in the promised land. We are on our way to it, and that promise will be fully fulfilled when Christ returns or when we are with the Lord forever.

Meanwhile, we go through seasons. We go through dark places. We are going through times of struggle, spiritual warfare, fatigue and uncertainty. Not because God is not there, but because we are not home yet.

But we walk with a firm certainty: the victory has already been won by Christ. He conquered sin. He conquered death. He conquered what we could never have conquered. That's why we can trust.

That is why the Lord tells us: "Fear not, little flock; for it is the Father's pleasure to give you the kingdom" (Luke 12:32). And it also reminds us:

"Let not your heart be troubled: believe in God, believe also in me. In my Father's house there are many mansions [...] I go to prepare a place for you. And if it is for me, and I prepare a place for you, I will come again and will take you to myself: so that where I am, you may be also." John 14:1-3

We're not home yet. We are on the road. We are crossing the desert. But God is with us, like a cloud and like fire, guiding us to the end.
