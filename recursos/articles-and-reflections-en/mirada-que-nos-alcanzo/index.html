A look that reached us before we existed

About Ephesians 1:4–7 and Ephesians 2:11–13.

There are times when reading Scripture with too many glasses causes us to lose our sight. Modern lenses. Doctrinal lenses. Lenses of systems that try to explain mechanisms, but that often end up extinguishing the astonishment that the Holy Spirit wanted to provoke.

For this reason, the Word should not be read from modern categories, nor from closed systems that seek to explain how God works, but from the place where the text wanted to speak to us: the heart of the Father.

When the church in Ephesus first heard these words, they were not trying to figure out a salvation mechanism. She was being confronted with an infinitely deeper reality: that God was not late to her story.

Paul reminded them that there was a time when they were without God and without hope in the world:

"Therefore, remember that once you Gentiles in the flesh [...] were without Christ, alienated from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers to the covenants of promise, without hope and without God in the world. But now in Christ Jesus, you who once were far away have been made near by the blood of Christ." Ephesians 2:11-13

Outside the pact. Far. Foreigners. Dead in crimes and sins. Slaves inside, even knowing they were. With a captive conscience, with no real way out, no strength to save themselves.

And from that place—not from virtue, not from merit—the text raises its gaze toward God. A holy God. Eternal. Perfect. Who has seen all of human history and all its evil. And yet... he did not abandon us.

This is where the text stops being an explanation and becomes a revelation.

Sometimes we are touched to know that our earthly parents love us, and that is beautiful. But what Scripture reveals to us is something infinitely greater: that there is a Father in heaven, holier, purer, more glorious and more perfect, who looked at us before the foundation of the world and did not reject us.

"As he chose us in him before the foundation of the world, that we should be holy and blameless before him in love [...] to the praise of the glory of his grace, whereby he made us accepted in the Beloved: in whom we have redemption through his blood, the remission of sins through the riches of his grace." Ephesians 1:4-7

He didn't see us with surprise. He did not see us with contempt. He did not see us with indifference. He looked at us with love.

And that is what it means to have been thought of before, known before, considered before. Not a cold decree. Not a mechanical destiny. But a mercy that anticipated disaster.

A God who, seeing our slavery, decided to extend his hand. A God who, seeing our death, decided to pay the price. A God who, seeing that there was no way out, decided to become the way out himself.

He did not leave us in a vacuum. He didn't leave us alone. He did not leave us in death. He did everything he needed to do to solve a problem we couldn't solve. He carried the weight himself. He paid the debt himself.

And this is a truth that every believer can make his own and say in his heart: God saw me when he could not save me. God thought of me when I had no hope. God extended his hand from before to not let me lose.

Therefore, when we talk about election, and when we talk about a foreknowledge of God, the church is not called to discuss systems, nor to fight between determinisms or human ambiguities. The church is called to worship.

Because what we see in the text is not a God who controls from afar, but a Father who loves from before. Not a God who reacts late, but one who never lost sight of his children.

Reading Scripture without imposing modern lenses on it does not impoverish us; It gives us back the message that the Holy Spirit wanted to give from the beginning: that salvation was not born in us, that hope did not spring from our strength, that life reached us because God decided not to let us go.

And when the church understands this, it stops looking for mechanisms and learns to rest in grace. Because she knows that, if she is alive today, it is because a hand of love extended from before and found her right where there was no way out.
