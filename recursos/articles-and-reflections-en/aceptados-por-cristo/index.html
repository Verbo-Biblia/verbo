Not accepted because we conquered, but because Christ conquered

Today I want to speak with a pastoral heart, not to justify sin, but neither to crush those who fight.

"If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and there is no truth in us. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and cleanse us from all unrighteousness." 1 John 1:8-9

This was not written for unbelievers, but for real believers, for children of God who walk, stumble, get up and keep walking.

Many of us sincerely love Christ. We love our families, we serve, we pray, we study the Word, we seek to reflect Jesus in our character and in our way of living. And yet, there are struggles that don't seem to disappear as quickly as we would like. Recurring setbacks. Sins that we hate, but against which we continue to battle.

And this is where we must affirm a truth that sustains the soul: we are not accepted by God because we conquered today, we are accepted because Christ conquered for us.

Our peace with God is not gained every morning by good behavior, nor lost every night by a fall. Peace with God was established by the work of Christ, not by our daily performance.

"Therefore justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ." Romans 5:1

Now, this grace is not permission to live comfortably in sin. On the contrary. Precisely because we have been accepted, forgiven and loved, we cannot make peace with that which destroys us. Grace does not teach us to justify sin, but to hate it without hating ourselves.

Scripture calls us not only to put off the old man, but to put on the new:

"To leave, as to the former way of life, the old man that is corrupted according to the desires of error; and to be renewed in the spirit of his mind, and to put on the new man that is created according to God in righteousness and holiness of truth." Ephesians 4:22-24

It is not enough to leave sin; It is necessary to fill life with new paths, with healthy habits, with an ordered life before God. Sin is not only desired: it is often learned. And what is learned is unlearned by walking in a different way, with patience, with truth and with support.

If you are struggling today, I want to tell you something with pastoral clarity: your pain over sin is not a sign of condemnation, it is evidence that the Holy Spirit is working in you. A hardened heart does not suffer like this. A transformed heart yes.

"For godly pain works healthy repentance, which need not be repented of; but eternal pain works death." 2 Corinthians 7:10

Maybe this fight will not disappear overnight. Maybe it won't be a short battle. But that doesn't mean God isn't working in you. Sanctification is not always quick, nor linear, nor visible in all areas at the same time. But it always has a direction: towards Christ.

We don't celebrate sin here. We don't soften it here. But we also do not expel those who fight and repent. We walk together, we confess, we rise, and we continue to move forward, trusting not in our strength, but in the grace that sustains us.

You are not accepted because you won today. You are accepted because Christ conquered for you. And precisely for that reason, you will not stop fighting against what wants to destroy you.

May the Lord grant us a sensitive heart, a healthy conscience, a firm faith in His grace, and a humble determination to continue growing in holiness, without masks and without fear. May the peace of God, which depends not on our performance but on His faithfulness, guard our hearts and minds in Christ Jesus.
